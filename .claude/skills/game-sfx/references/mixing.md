# Mixing the game: levels, categories, defaults, fatigue

How the SFX mix of PROJECT GGG is built and how to keep it healthy. The numbers come from
measuring real bot matches (`scripts/capture.mjs` + `scripts/mixstats.py`) and from the sources
listed at the end.

## 1. What went wrong in the first mix

A 75 s bot match recorded at the old defaults (master 0.8, music 0.45, sfx 0.9, linear sliders):

| Measure | Value | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| Master integrated | -13.3 LUFS, peaks -1.4 dBFS | 5 dB too loud, limiter always working |
| Music vs SFX | music only 2.6 dB under SFX integrated | SFX events had no room |
| Band balance | 20-120 Hz was the strongest band | sub that laptops cannot play pumped the limiter |
| `hit` (2.6 plays/s) | -15 LUFS-M, 10 dB above swings, distorted 165 -> 54 Hz sine | the "annoying" hit: loud, buzzy, boomy |
| Own hit confirm | square wave 2.6-3.9 kHz, stacked on the 3D hit and a 5-9 kHz crit sparkle | piercing, three sounds per hit |
| Noise sounds | boost, gas, footsteps, UI: flatness 0.15-0.34, centroid 5-6 kHz | "too much noise" |
| Rapid fire | Rex's shot 2.75/s arena-wide, Sera's beam a sound every 0.1 s | machine-gun clutter |
| Trigger density | 21 SFX/s in the arena, 10.5/s within 25 m | everything audible from everywhere |

## 2. Architecture: normalise every sound, then mix by category

Two separate jobs, never mixed up:

1. **Asset normalisation** (`src/core/audio/trims.ts`, generated by `tools/audio/mix_trims.mjs`):
   every SFX is rendered dry, its momentary loudness measured (BS.1770 K-weighted, 400 ms,
   LUFS-M) and a trim stored so that it plays at `REF_LUFS` at full volume and 0 m. A recipe only
   has to sound right; it never has to be "at the right level". Re-run the tool after changing
   a recipe (`node tools/audio/mix_trims.mjs kaiser.` for one champion).
2. **The mix** (`src/core/audio/mix.ts`): every SFX names a category; the category has a level in
   dB, a distance range, a voice budget, a bus EQ and a reverb send. A sound's `lvl` is only its
   rank inside the category (a light cast -3, a finisher +2). Changing one category level moves
   all of its sounds together, which is the only sane way to balance hundreds of sounds.

Signal flow: voice (trim, `lvl`, distance) -> air low-pass -> pan -> category bus (level, EQ,
duck) -> SFX slider -> light glue (2:1 above -15 dBFS) -> master slider -> safety limiter
(-2 dBFS, 20:1). The score has its own slider and sits `MUSIC_DB` under the SFX in combat,
`MUSIC_MENU_DB` higher in menus and results.

## 3. The category table

| Category | dB | Range (m) | Voices | Bus EQ | What lives there |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ult | 0 | 10-110 | 4 | hp 35 | ultimate casts, threat cues, ult payoffs |
| big | -2 | 8-85 | 5 | hp 40 | explosions, slams, parry, deaths |
| skill | -5 | 6-60 | 8 | hp 70 | signature / secondary / special casts, procs, channels |
| impact | -8 | 5-50 | 6 | hp 70, lp 12k | a hit landing on a body (one per hit) |
| basic | -11 | 5-45 | 8 | hp 90, lp 12k | swings, shots, orbs: the most frequent combat sounds |
| move | -14 | 4-35 | 8 | hp 90, lp 11k | hooks, gas, dashes, landings, the wind/jet/cable loops |
| foley | -21 | 3-22 | 6 | hp 120 | footsteps, jumps |
| confirm | -12 | 2D | 4 | hp 200, lp 9k | own hit/kill confirms, medals, timing ticks (local player only) |
| hurt | -12 | 2D | 2 | lp 6k | the local player taking a hit |
| ui | -15 | 2D | 3 | hp 200 | menus |

Why this order: what the player must react to sits on top (ultimates, explosions, ability casts
aimed at them); the most frequent sounds (basic attacks, impacts) sit lower because frequency
multiplies their weight in the mix and the ear tires of them first; traversal and foley are a bed
so eight fighters grappling never become a wall of noise; personal confirms are quiet but 2D and
never attenuated, so the attacker always hears a hit land; UI is the quietest. The engine drops
confirm-category sounds that come from another fighter (their perfect timing is not yours).

## 4. Distance, voices, rate limits

- **Distance**: full level up to `near`, then inverse distance (-6 dB per doubling), faded to
  silence over the last 30 % of the range. Ranges are per category: a footstep is gone at 22 m,
  an ultimate carries across the arena. The old curve (`1/(1+0.045 d)`) left everything audible
  at 45 m: that was half of the clutter.
- **Air and space**: low-pass from 20 kHz falling with distance past `near` (floor 15 %), behind
  the head x0.6; reverb send grows with distance; own sounds drier and narrower.
- **Voice budget per category**: when full, the quietest voice is dropped, or the new one if it
  is the quietest (own sounds always get in). Plus `max` per sound (oldest stolen).
- **Rate limits**: identical sounds from one source merge within 25 ms; `gap` on a definition
  sets the minimum time between plays from one source (channels, buzz grains, ticks); impacts
  have their own `gap` per ability (Rex's shots 0.2 s, so every other bullet of a 7/s stream
  lands an impact; Sera's beam ticks 0.3 s; bee stings 0.25 s; bat storm 0.45 s; honey puddle
  0.95 s) and one impact per victim per 40 ms, the heavier one winning. A passive or proc that
  lands in the same frame as the hit gets a lighter weight than the hit so only one impact plays
  (Locke's stake, Qiyana's Royal Privilege), and carries its own cue instead (`qiyana.royal`).
- **Who makes a sound**: `play(name, pos, vol, src)` with the fighter as `src` when the sound is
  not at the fighter (a pillar at the target point, a projectile bursting, a blink's arrival).
  Without it the engine assumes the nearest fighter within 2.5 m, which credits a pillar landing
  on you to you: your own-sound treatment, no enemy threat layer, the wrong champion's flavour.
  A champion id as `src` only sets the flavour (impacts sound like the attacker's weapon).
  Confirm and UI sounds from another fighter are dropped (a bot's "nope" is not yours to hear).

## 5. Hits: one impact + one confirm, in the ability's material

`Match.applyDamage` calls `audio.impact(...)` once per damage event; `Match.hitFeedback` calls
`audio.confirm(...)` once for the local attacker. Nothing else plays on a hit.

- **World impact** (`impacts.ts`, 3D at the victim): the material comes from the ability
  (`IMPACTS[champion]["slot.part"]`: blade, holo, greatsword, ringblade, claw, stake, nail,
  blunt, bullet, slug, sonic, shock, aoe, bite, bat, honey, sting, rock, water, grass, holy, and
  a separate `hit.block`). When the ability's own sound already is the blast (an ultimate
  landing, a wall bursting), its bodies take `aoe`: a thud each, not a second explosion. Weight `w` (0..1) from the ability, the damage and crit/counter sizes the
  body and the tail; crits add a metallic accent instead of a second sound.
- **Confirm** (2D, local attacker): a soft mallet tock on the current chord that climbs with the
  combo; crit = two tones ending on a bell; counter = a rising arpeggio; block = a muted tuk;
  momentum adds a swish; streams play it at -5 dB at most every 0.2 s; the kill is one chord stab
  with a bell (it replaced two stacked sounds).
- **Hurt** (2D, local victim): a body blow felt more than heard; the attacker's impact plays at
  0.6 under it.
- Impact anatomy: clean pitched body 150-500 Hz with a fast pitch fall, a band-passed pink smack
  0.6-1.8 kHz, a 5 ms click at low level, crumble and sub only when heavy. 60-250 ms. No drive on
  the body (the old distorted sine was the buzz people hated).

## 6. Loudness target, music balance, default sliders, slider curve

- **Target**: about -18 LUFS integrated for the whole game in combat at default settings, true
  peak under -1 dBTP (safety limiter at -2 dBFS working < 10 % of the time). That is the
  "portable/PC" end of the published targets (console -24 LKFS +-2 by Sony ASWG-R001, mobile and
  portable -16 to -18) because a browser game plays on laptop speakers next to other tabs.
  Measured after the rework with all nine kits, two 75 s bot matches at default settings
  (Kaiser on neon_city against Nova, Rex, Sera, Akali, Qiyana; Rex on stage against Locke, Pooh,
  Elisabbat, Kaiser, Sera): master -18.1 LUFS both, limiter active 2.0 % / 1.2 % (never more
  than 3 dB), glue active 14 %, 7.3-7.9 sounds/s within 25 m. SFX bus bands, before -> after:
  20-120 Hz -2.2 -> -6.7 dB, 500-2000 Hz -13.8 -> -6.9, 2-5 kHz -12.7 -> -18, 5-9 kHz
  -21.8 -> -30, 9-20 kHz -25.8 -> -40: the weight moved from sub and hiss into the body.
- **Music balance**: in combat the score sits around -22 LUFS integrated (stereo), level with
  the SFX integrated (within 2 dB) while SFX events peak about 7 dB above it (momentary); menus
  +3 dB (no SFX to make room for). Before the rework the SFX sat 2.6 dB over the score
  integrated and 6.5 dB over it 95 % of the time (a constant wall); now 2.6 dB at the 95th
  percentile and about 7 dB on the biggest events, so the action stands out when it matters. Big moments duck
  it 2-5 dB for ~0.2 s plus a 0.6 s release; ultimates and explosions also duck the basic, move
  and foley buses 3-4 dB.
- **Defaults**: master 100 %, SFX 100 %, music 85 %: the mix is designed at the top of the
  sliders so players only ever turn things down, and music starts slightly under the SFX because
  it is the first thing people lower.
- **Slider curve**: ears hear level in dB, so a linear gain slider puts all the action in its last
  quarter. The slider walks a 40 dB scale (`gain = 10^((s-1)*40/20)`, linear fade below 5 %,
  0 = silence): 50 % = -20 dB, 85 % = -6 dB.
- **Migration**: settings saved with the old linear sliders move to the new defaults when they
  were never touched, and keep their ratio to the old default when they were (half as loud
  stays half as loud); 0 stays muted (`Settings.ts`, `audioMix: 2`).

## 7. Noise and fatigue rules for synthesized SFX

- Attacks up to 8 ms rise linearly on the SFX graph (`Graph.softAttack`). An exponential ramp
  from silence is inaudible for three quarters of its length and then jumps: a 2 ms attack acts
  like 0.2 ms, a click reaching into 2-5 kHz on every sine body. Measured on the same sounds:
  `hurt` -14 dB less 2-5 kHz, footsteps -7 dB, confirms and UI -5 to -8 dB above 5 kHz, loudness
  within 1 dB. The music keeps its own envelopes (it was tuned with them).
- Noise is pink or brown and band-limited; white noise only as the 1-2 ms edge of a click, never
  a body or a tail. Sustained energy above ~6 kHz is hiss.
- The 2-5 kHz band (ear's peak sensitivity) is for short definition, not bodies: sounds heard
  several times a second keep most of their energy in 120 Hz-2 kHz.
- Transients 2-15 ms and quiet next to the body (`punch - lufsM` under ~9 dB in `sfxstats.py`).
- Sub (< 80 Hz) only on ultimates and big explosions; character bass an octave up (oct 2-3)
  where laptops can play it and the score's bass is not.
- Repetition is the main fatigue: every play varies (pitch, grain, filter) and repeats escalate
  only where it means something (combos).
- Bright tonal confirms go on chord tones at octave 5 (500 Hz-1.2 kHz) with soft mallet partials,
  not squares at 3-4 kHz.
- Watch the opposite failure too: with almost nothing left in 2-5 kHz the mix turns dull and
  muffled; keep each impact's smack and each cast's edge.

## 8. The checking loop

```bash
npx vite --port 5199 &                                                  # once
node tools/audio/mix_trims.mjs rex.                                     # normalise after edits
node .claude/skills/game-sfx/scripts/render.mjs sfxDry '{"names":["rex.shot"],"repeat":3}' /tmp/a/dry
python3 .claude/skills/game-sfx/scripts/sfxstats.py /tmp/a/dry           # per-sound loudness/timbre
node .claude/skills/game-sfx/scripts/capture.mjs 'http://localhost:5199/?play&champ=rex&bots=5&diff=0.8' 60 /tmp/a/match
python3 .claude/skills/game-sfx/scripts/mixstats.py /tmp/a/match         # the whole mix in a match
```

## 9. Sources

- Sony Interactive Entertainment, Audio Standards Working Group, ASWG-R001 loudness
  recommendation: -24 LKFS +-2 LU for home consoles, -18 LKFS for portable systems, -1 dBTP;
  its survey found 52 games averaging -17.8 LUFS against -23.1 for films
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_normalization,
  https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2013/slides/824764Garry_Taylor_Loudness_and_how.pdf,
  https://youlean.co/loudness-standards-full-comparison-table/).
- ITU-R BS.1770-4 (K-weighting, gating) and EBU R128 / Tech 3341 (momentary 400 ms, short-term 3 s).
- Rob Bridgett, "Game Audio Mixing" (snapshots, ducking, priority, the mix as a design tool).
- Scott Lawlor & Tomas Neumann, GDC 2016, "Overwatch: The Elusive Goal - Play by Sound"
  (pillars: a clear mix, pinpoint accuracy, gameplay information; a dynamic priority on the
  greatest threats instead of plain HDR; enemy and friendly sounds treated differently)
  (https://www.audiokinetic.com/en/blog/overwatch-game-audio-using-wwise-1/,
  https://pcgamesn.com/overwatch/overwatch-devs-on-creating-a-game-you-can-play-by-sound-and-announcing-dolby-atmos-support).
- DICE / Frostbite HDR audio (loudness window driven by the loudest sounds).
- Riot Games, "Ask VALORANT" (2020): footsteps are mixed to be heard rather than to portray
  distance (a flat attenuation curve) so ability noise never masks an approaching enemy, the
  game is mixed in stereo, and the mix should never push players to raise their volume
  (https://tracker.gg/valorant/articles/riot-responds-to-complaints-over-footstep-audio-in-valorant).
- Music under SFX by default: developers commonly ship the music 10-20 % under the effects so
  the effects are not drowned (https://babel.speldesign.uu.se/?p=1998); players who meet all
  sliders at 100 % turn the music down first
  (https://www.neogaf.com/threads/sound-settings-preferences.910724/).
- Volume sliders in dB: map the slider exponentially (constant dB per step) with true silence at
  the bottom (https://music.arts.uci.edu/dobrian/webaudio/tutorials/JavaScript/volumefaderdbjs.html);
  a pure dB scale never reaches zero, hence a linear tail near the bottom
  (https://hydrogenaudio.org/index.php/topic,86414.0.html,
  https://forums.cherryaudio.com/viewtopic.php?p=14509). Dr. Lex, "Programming Volume
  Controls", is the classic write-up of the same mapping. The game uses 40 dB rather than a
  hi-fi 50-60 dB range because only the top 20-30 dB of a game mix is ever useful: 50 % lands
  at -20 dB instead of an almost silent -30.
